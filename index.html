01 / Selected work

PartnerPath
A clearer onboarding journey.

Partners need to provide business information, connect systems, prepare articles and complete launch checks. The product challenge is helping them move independently through that complexity while preserving readiness standards.

BUSINESS GOAL

Earlier readiness to sell

Reduce avoidable onboarding delays and repeated specialist coordination.

USER GOAL

Know what to do next

Understand the task, complete it correctly and see what unlocks progress.

MY PRODUCT BET

Guide before escalating

Combine visible next steps with actionable validation and an accessible specialist.

Confidentiality note: PartnerPath is a portfolio recreation of the product journey, not the original Zalando system. Company and partner records, internal screens, credentials, architecture and confidential performance data are omitted or replaced. Proposed improvements are identified separately.

Discovery / From experience to product

Understand the people.
Then choose what to solve.

As a Product Manager working on partner onboarding at Zalando, I worked with colleagues on discovery for the portal. The work connected partner needs with the business goal of helping retailers become ready to sell with less confusion and repeated coordination.

01 / DISCOVER

Map the partner journey

Bring together partner workshop findings, user roles and the steps between handover and launch. Identify who supplies information, who completes a task and who needs help.

02 / FRAME

Find the repeated friction

Partners could not always see their next step, understand technical requirements or know whether submitted information was correct. Repeated clarification slowed progress.

03 / PRIORITISE

Focus on guided progress

Turn those needs into a clearer onboarding journey with requirements, task guidance and visible readiness. Keep specialist support available when partners cannot continue independently.

Original product work at Zalando

From discovery to a portal partners could use

The discovery findings informed the onboarding portal: what information partners needed to provide, which tasks came next and how the journey could help them prepare for launch. We validated the original portal with partners and received positive feedback.

This account describes the product work without publishing internal research artifacts, detailed implementation information or confidential results.

Portfolio development today

Recreate the decisions in a working prototype

I translated the journey into generic requirements and fictional partner scenarios. I used Google AI Studio and AI-assisted coding to build the screens, then iterated on bulk upload, test orders, activation, empty states and clearer language.

I kept the original product experience distinct from new proposals such as the guidance assistant. The published demo makes those ideas tangible without exposing the original system.

05 / Evidence and next decision

Make the outcome
measurable.

A polished prototype is evidence that I can make a solution concrete. It is not evidence that the solution has improved onboarding performance. These are the measures I would use to decide whether to invest further.

Time to activation

How long it takes a partner to complete onboarding and activate a store

Independent task completion

Can a partner finish a task without specialist assistance?

First submission acceptance

How often is submitted data accepted without correction?

Coordination effort

Support contacts and specialist time per partner

Launch quality

Test failures, premature activation and first-week order failures

06 / How I work

I make product
decisions visible.

My role is to connect the user problem to a business goal, choose a useful scope and bring the solution far enough that people can test it.

In this case that meant clear user roles, explicit priorities, controlled launch gates and a working prototype. I chose depth in one partner journey before adding a separate manager product or daily order operations.
